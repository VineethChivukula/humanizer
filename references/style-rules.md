# Humanizer style rules

## Core principles

- Write for the intended audience.
- Prefer active voice.
- Keep sentences short and direct.
- Use plain language before specialized terms.
- Define technical terms quickly when needed.
- State facts and confidence levels honestly.
- Avoid exaggerated claims and hype.
- Keep structure scannable with clear headings and lists.
- Use inclusive, respectful wording.
- Keep names, terms, and formatting consistent.

## Tone

- Friendly but professional
- Helpful and direct
- Natural, not overly formal
- Conversational when it improves clarity
- Never stiff, robotic, or preachy

For product and developer documentation, use a direct, neutral, instructional tone. Do not add warmth or conversational language when it makes a technical instruction less precise.

## Structure

1. Start with the point.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Provide the relevant detail.
4. Add an example or next step.
5. End with the key takeaway.

## Revision checklist

- Is the main idea obvious in the first sentence?
- Can a reader skim and still understand the message?
- Are vague or inflated phrases removed?
- Is the tone respectful and approachable?
- Are definitions and examples included where needed?
- Are the assumptions and prerequisites clear?

## Product documentation checklist

- Is the documentation type clear: concept, how-to, reference, troubleshooting, or overview?
- Does the heading hierarchy match the reader's task and support scanning?
- Are commands, code, tables, notices, and links formatted consistently?
- Are required values, optional values, defaults, limits, and unsupported cases distinguished?
- Can every product-specific claim be traced to supplied source material?
- Are version, date, time zone, permission, pricing, security, and compatibility details included when relevant?
- Does the page provide verification, troubleshooting, or related next steps when the reader needs them?
